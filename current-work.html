01 · Research paper · Ghana–China relations

From Go Out to Galamsey: Chinese Mining and the Politics of Environmental Sovereignty in Ghana, 2008–2025

The usual question

Why did Ghana struggle to enforce its mining laws against Chinese miners?

Scholars usually answer with informality, migration, patronage, or dependence on China.

My question

How did enforcement itself become something Ghana and China negotiated?

Ghana exercised real environmental sovereignty, but selectively, within limits set by its economic ties to China.

Damage to farmland, rivers, and water supplies drove large crackdowns in 2013, 2017, and 2025. Chinese officials objected to how Ghana enforced the law, including the treatment of detainees, “forcible measures,” and media portrayals, but they never publicly challenged Ghana’s right to enforce it. Arrests of ordinary miners continued at scale. Restraint appeared only in visible cases, most clearly the 2018 dropping of Aisha Huang’s prosecution, which a senior minister later linked to Chinese infrastructure financing.

By 2025 the two governments were sorting Chinese participation into two tracks. That arrangement changed the relationship without solving the environmental problem. The paper also treats U.S. technical assistance as a short case, and it adds a Ghanaian case to debates on African agency and resource sovereignty.

2013

Large crackdown on illegal mining

2017

Another large crackdown

2025

New crackdown and a two-track arrangement: deportations for illegal miners, a formal China–Ghana mining association for established firms

02 · Research paper · Political economy

“Dignify Your Position and That of Posterity”: Cocoa Farmers and the Politics of Price in the Gold Coast, 1930–1948

From 1930 to 1948, Gold Coast cocoa farmers fought what one of their spokesmen called “the battle for price”: first against the European firms’ buying agreements and, after 1939, against the colonial state itself.

Historians have usually treated the hold-ups of 1930–31 and 1937–38 as commercial disputes, as contests driven by chiefs, brokers, and wealthy producers, or as the prelude to state-controlled marketing. Histories of nationalism have cast farmers as a pool of grievance that urban parties later mobilized. I read farmers’ collective action instead as a sustained body of political-economic argument.

When the state replaced the merchant firms as the only buyer, farmers’ claims turned the price of cocoa into a question of public authority, and finally of self-government. By 1948, they had developed an economic nationalism that came before the mass parties and supplied the terms those parties later used to appeal to the countryside.

Drawn from Ghana’s national archives

Petitions, meeting reports, leaflets, union minutes, and commission evidence.

03 · Policy paper · Climate & public health

Heat Training for New Jersey Frontline Groups: Existing Programs, Enforcement Levers, and a Shared Toolkit

The strongest way to prepare community leaders for heat waves is to add heat to training people already have to take, rather than build new programs. New Jersey already has a working model: every school coach must renew a free heat-illness course each year.

I map existing training for coaches, outdoor workers, youth camps, childcare staff, teachers, and aging-care facilities. In most of these sectors, heat is not a required topic. Because NJDEP cannot mandate training in other sectors, I rank the levers available to it, then propose a shared 30-minute core toolkit with short add-ons for each sector.

Sectors mapped

Coaches Outdoor workers Youth camps Childcare staff Teachers Aging-care facilities

Levers, from strongest to weakest

Strongest

Add heat to training that other agencies already require.

Next

Add a heat line to existing inspections, and count heat training toward credits people already need.

Then

Support pending bills on worker heat safety and classroom temperatures, and offer incentives to groups that face no rules.

The proposal

A shared 30-minute core toolkit, with short add-ons for each sector.
